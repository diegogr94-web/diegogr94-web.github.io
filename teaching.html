Aristotle (PHL304)Upper-year undergraduate course · University of Toronto · Winter 2027

We start with the Physics, where Aristotle keeps deferring questions to a ‘first’ philosophy, and then follow him through the Metaphysics as he works out what that science is and what it studies: being qua being, substance, actuality and, finally, an eternal unmoved mover. We end with the De Anima and the Nicomachean Ethics, asking what all this means for the nature of the soul and the best human life.

Early Greek Philosophy (PHL301)Upper-year undergraduate course · University of Toronto · Fall 2026

Looking past the influential reports of Plato and Aristotle, we read the Presocratics and the Sophists as deep and creative thinkers in their own right. Since the evidence survives only in fragments, much of our work consists in reconstructing what they thought about nature, being, knowledge and human life, while always asking how far the evidence really lets us piece their theories together.

Cosmology and Politics in Plato’s Timaeus and CritiasUndergraduate seminar, taught in German · LMU Munich · Summer 2025

We read the Timaeus and Critias side by side, following Plato’s account of the cosmos as the work of a divine craftsman together with the political story that frames it: the city of the Republic, seemingly realized in ancient Athens, its war against Atlantis, and the place of human beings in the cosmos.
