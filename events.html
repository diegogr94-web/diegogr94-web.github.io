Political Knowledge in Plato

International workshop · University of Toronto · 7 November 2025

Myrthe Bartels and I brought together a small group of scholars to ask what political knowledge is in Plato, who characterizes it in different and sometimes seemingly incompatible ways: here as metaphysical insight, there as a trained craft. After a keynote by Melissa Lane, we spent the day on the Laws and the Republic, closing with a panel on the Statesman with Melissa Lane, Rachel Barney and Ryan Balot, which I moderated.

Metaphysics and Politics across the History of Philosophy

Joint LMU Munich – UC Berkeley conference · LMU Munich · 30–31 May 2025

Sophie Kikkert and I organized this joint conference of LMU Munich and UC Berkeley to ask how views about the nature of reality shape political thinking, and whether they should. The first day turned to contemporary questions, with a panel on the fact/value distinction with Peter Adamson and Alyssa Ney, moderated by Sophie; the second went back through the history of philosophy, including talks on Aristotle by Christof Rapp and on Spinoza by Kristin Primus.

Craft and Nature in Plato and Aristotle

International workshop · University of Toronto · 6–7 March 2025

Rareș Marinescu and I organized two days on how Plato and Aristotle use the language and conceptual structure of craft to think about nature, with talks by Mary-Louise Gill, Rachana Kamtekar, and Caleb Cohoe, among others. The papers took up Aristotle’s account of intellect, the status of artefacts, teleology, the Gorgias, and the puppet image of the soul in the Laws.

A Language for Spatio-Temporal Entities in Plato’s Timaeus?

International workshop · Ruhr-University Bochum · 26–27 October 2023

Anna Pavani and I organized this workshop around one of the hardest passages in the Timaeus, where Plato asks how we can speak about things that never stop changing. It opened with a keynote by Barbara Sattler and a roundtable on language and perceptible things; on the second day we read the text closely together and heard papers by Federico Petrucci and Samuel Meister, with responses.
